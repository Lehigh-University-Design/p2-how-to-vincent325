Planning Your Trip

Check Your Route

The NYC Subway runs 24/7 across 472 stations on 25 routes with 665 miles of track. Before heading out, use the MTA app or Google Maps to plan your journey. Express trains skip stations, so check if you need local or express service.

Quick Tips:

  • Check for delays or service changes. Usually posted on the MTA website or Google Maps
  • Check weekend service changes on mta.info
  • The MTA map is available at most stations and on train cars if needed

The MTA app shows real-time arrivals

Payment Methods

Paying for the subway with OMNY

Ways to Pay

The fare is $2.90 per ride. You can pay using OMNY (tap your phone or contactless card) or a MetroCard. OMNY automatically caps weekly fares at $34 after 12 rides, making it the most convenient option for visitors and regular riders alike.

Payment Options:

  • OMNY: Tap your phone (Apple/Google Pay) or contactless credit/debit card directly on the reader
  • MetroCard: Purchase at station booths or machines. Add money or unlimited rides (7-day for $34, 30-day for $132)
  • Free transfers: You get one free transfer between subway and bus within 2 hours
  • Reduced fare: Available for seniors (65+) and people with disabilities

Navigating the Station

Finding Your Way

Each station has clear signage to help you navigate. Look for train line indicators (numbers or letters in colored circles) and directional signs. Pay attention to whether you need an Uptown (north) or Downtown (south) platform before you swipe in.

Navigation Tips:

  • Platform direction: Uptown/Bronx goes north, Downtown/Brooklyn goes south
  • Express vs Local: Express (diamond symbol) skips stations, Local (circle) stops at all stations
  • Transfers: Look for black and white "Transfer" signs to connect between lines
  • Exit signs: Green signs show street exits, follow them to avoid getting lost
  • Platform signs: Digital boards show arrival times and service alerts

Riding Etiquette & Safety

A typical subway car during rush hour

Be a Good Rider

The subway moves 5.5 million people daily. Following basic etiquette makes the ride better for everyone. Move to the center of the car, let people exit before boarding, and keep your belongings close. Stand clear of closing doors - forcing them causes delays for everyone.

Etiquette & Safety:

  • Priority seating: Give up your seat for elderly, pregnant, or disabled riders
  • Backpacks: Remove and hold them by your feet during crowded rides
  • Music & calls: Use headphones and keep conversations quiet
  • Stand right, walk left: On escalators, stand to the right
  • Stay alert: Keep valuables secure and be aware of your surroundings
  • Emergency: Use the intercom button to contact the conductor

Dealing with Service Changes

When Things Don't Go as Planned

Service changes are common, especially on weekends and late nights. The MTA performs maintenance regularly, and unexpected delays can happen. Stay informed through the MTA app, station announcements, or service advisory posters to avoid getting stranded.

Handling Disruptions:

  • Weekend service: Many lines run differently on weekends - always check before traveling
  • Real-time updates: MTA app and Google Maps show live service status
  • Alternative routes: Learn multiple ways to reach your destination
  • Service advisories: Orange posters at stations explain planned work
  • Delays: "Signal problems" and "train traffic" are common - be patient
  • Late night: Some stations close; service runs less frequently after midnight
