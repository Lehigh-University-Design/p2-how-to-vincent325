Planning Your Trip

Check Your Route

The NYC Subway runs 24/7 across 472 stations on 25 routes with 665 miles of track. Before heading out, use the MTA app or Google Maps to plan your journey. Express trains skip stations, so check if you need local or express service.

Quick Tips:

  • Check for delays or service changes. Usually posted on the MTA website or Google Maps
  • Check weekend service changes on mta.info
  • The MTA map is available at most stations and on train cars if needed

Ways to Pay

Tap a contactless card, phone, or watch at any turnstile. After 12 rides in a week, the rest are free (capped at $34).

IMPORTANT: Metrocards sales and refills are ending December 31, 2025. Starting January 2026, fares are also going up to $3.00 from $2.90

Best Option for Most Riders:

  • OMNY (Tap to Pay): Use any contactless card or digital wallet. No setup needed.
  • MetroCard: Buy at vending machines. Consider the 7-day unlimited ($34) if you'll ride 13+ times.
  • Free transfers: Switch between subway and bus within 2 hours at no extra charge.

Navigating the Station

Find Your Platform

Check the train line (letter/number) and direction before entering the turnstile. Some stations have separate entrances for each direction—you can't switch sides without paying again.

Key Things to Know:

  • Uptown = North (toward the Bronx), Downtown = South (toward Brooklyn). Manhattan is in between.
  • Stand back from the platform edge, especially when trains arrive.
  • Follow signs for your train line and direction. Look for colored circles matching your train.
  • Some stations have multiple levels or exits. Check signs carefully to avoid getting lost.

What to Expect

The subway moves 5.5 million people daily. Trains can be crowded during rush hours (7-9 AM, 5-7 PM) but usually arrive every 2-10 minutes. Modern cars have digital displays showing upcoming stops, while older cars have posted maps above the doors.

Practical Tips:

  • Let people exit first before you board. Stand to the side of the doors.
  • Move to the center of the car if it's crowded. Don't block the doors.
  • Hold your bag in front of you in crowded cars. Keep your phone in sight.
  • Stops and Maps are on most modern trains to help with navigation.

Getting Around the City

Major Destinations

Nearly every NYC attraction is near a subway stop. These key stations connect you to multiple lines and popular areas.

Essential Stops:

  • Times Square-42 St: Theater district, shopping. Transfer hub for 7 lines.
  • Grand Central-42 St: Historic terminal, Midtown offices. Access to 4/5/6/7/S trains.
  • 34 St-Penn Station: Major transit hub, near Madison Square Garden. Access to 1/2/3/A/C/E lines.
  • Cortlandt St (Oculus): Financial district, near World Trade Center. Access to 1/2/3/A/C/E lines.
  • Coney Island: Beach, boardwalk, amusement park. End of D/F/N/Q lines.
